Part B

Why This Work, and Why Us

PART BGrounding~25 minutes

◈ The last thing before the Checkpoint

You know what this work is now, and who it is for. This module answers the last question before you decide: why does GlowStrm do it? Not because accessibility is a growing market — though it is. Because when you look closely at what the standards actually say, our values are already sitting there, written in somebody else’s vocabulary.

Three words that get used interchangeably, and shouldn’t be.

W3C draws a line between three things, and the distinction is worth carrying into every client conversation you will ever have.

AccessibilityDisabled people can equally perceive, understand, navigate, and interact — and contribute without barriers.
UsabilityA product is effective, efficient, and satisfying to use. Often researched without disabled people in the room.
InclusionEveryone is involved to the greatest extent possible — the widest of the three.

Read W3C’s own definition of inclusion

Here is what W3C lists under inclusion. Read it slowly:

Access to hardware, software, and connectivity. Computer literacy and skills. Economic situation. Education. Geographic location. Culture. Age. Language.

W3C WAI · Accessibility, Usability, and Inclusion · © W3C®

That is the global majority, written into a standards document. It is the list of who gets sorted out of the digital economy quietly — by infrastructure rather than by policy. And it is exactly who GlowStrm was built by and for.

We did not have to translate our first value into this field. It was already the field’s own definition. We center the global majority — not a footnote, our orientation.

Where the standard meets our values

Four more places the overlap is not a coincidence.

✦ Nothing changes until it is faced

W3C says something braver than most standards bodies say about themselves: when teams approach accessibility as a checklist, “the human interaction aspect is often lost, and accessibility is not achieved.”

That is our third value in their words. You can pass every automated scan and still ship a site nobody can use, because a checklist lets you look at the code instead of the person. We are witnesses first. Here that is not poetry — it is methodology.

✦ You are the authority of your own experience

W3C’s fix for that gap is to involve real disabled people early and throughout — not as testers at the end, but as authority from the start.

That is our fourth value, nearly verbatim. Lived experience is not anecdote; it is evidence. This field at its best already agrees with us. It just says “user research” where we say “witness is data.”

✦ Clarity is liberation

W3C: “Addressing accessibility at later stages becomes increasingly difficult.”

GlowStrm works upstream — before content, before campaigns, before execution. Accessibility behaves the same way. Retrofitting is expensive and partial; building it in is cheap and whole. That is not a slogan we are importing into this work. It is a description of how the work actually behaves.

✦ Your voice is not a problem to fix

One caution, because it is easy to get backwards. Accessibility asks for understandable content. That is not a mandate to flatten how anybody talks. Cognitive accessibility is about structure, predictability, and clear instruction — not about scrubbing rhythm or cadence out of a voice.

We speak simply because simplicity is respect. We speak clearly because clarity is care. Those are different from speaking blandly, and you will meet clients who confuse the two. You will know better.

The plainest version

Our second value says we will not participate in practices that extract, exploit, or exclude.

Web accessibility work is the removal of exclusion, one barrier at a time, paid for by the people who built it. There is no gap between the ethics and the invoice. That is rarer than it should be, and it is why this is the service we chose.

Private — for your notebook, not for submission. There are no right answers, and nobody sees these.

And if this isn’t your room

Here is the part most courses will not say. These values are a filter, not a welcome mat.

If you read the last few pages and felt yourself arguing — if centering the global majority sounds like politics rather than orientation, if choosing people over profit sounds naive, if the pace of care sounds like an excuse — that is useful information, and it arrived at exactly the right time.

This is the moment to step back, before the hours. Not because you failed anything. Because the fit is not there, and both of us are better off knowing now than three months in. Everything you have read so far is yours to keep — you can register an LLC, learn accessibility, and build this career entirely without us. Some of what is here will still serve you.

No hard feelings, ever. The door stays open if you change your mind.

And if you read those values and recognised yourself — welcome. The Checkpoint is next.

✦ The short version

Accessibility is about disability; usability is about whether a thing works well; inclusion is the widest circle of who gets left out.

W3C’s own inclusion list — economics, education, geography, culture, language — describes the global majority.

Checklists alone do not produce accessibility. Real people do. That is our methodology, not just our ethics.

This work removes exclusion and pays for it. There is no gap between the ethics and the invoice.
