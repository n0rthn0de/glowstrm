The Economic Case

Motivation that lasts is built on facts, not hype. So here are the numbers behind this work, plainly. Decide with your eyes open.

What it pays

The average U.S. salary for a Web Accessibility Specialist is about $91,584 (ZipRecruiter, 2025).

Freelance and consulting work commonly runs $37–$85 an hour, and credentialed experts regularly bill over $100 an hour.

A realistic note, because honesty is the point: those are mature numbers. Most people start lower — often with contract work in the lower end of that hourly range — and climb as the credentials and real projects stack up. The ceiling is high and the floor is livable. Both things are true.

Why the demand lasts

It’s remote-first — work you can do from anywhere, for clients anywhere.

It’s credential-driven — a certification you earn, not a network you’re born into, opens the door.

It’s recession-resistant — demand is created by law, not consumer mood. Companies fix accessibility because they’re required to.

It’s growing — the global digital accessibility market is projected to grow from $1.42 billion in 2025 to $3.24 billion by 2034, and the U.S. leads with about 36% of it.

And the pressure is rising in real time: ADA web-accessibility lawsuits rose 37% in the first half of 2025 alone. Every filing is a company that now has to pay someone to fix its website. That someone is increasingly people like you.

The throughline

This is a field where effort and credentials outweigh pedigree and connections — which is exactly why it works as a path to economic mobility. You are not asking anyone for a seat. You are earning a credential the market already needs.

Quick recap

Average specialist salary ~$91,584; freelance $37–$85/hr; experts $100/hr+.

Demand is remote, credential-driven, legally mandated, and growing — and accessibility lawsuits are climbing fast.

Credentials and effort matter more than pedigree here — that’s the whole opportunity.
