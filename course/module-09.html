The Pathway · Part C · Module 9

PART C · PHASE 1 · ~85 HOURS (SELF-PACED)

Phase 1: Web Accessibility Audits

This is the one that changes things. When you finish, you’ll hold a credential that lets you take on real, paid work: reviewing a client’s website for accessibility problems and delivering a clear report on how to fix them. That review is an audit — your first service offering.

What an audit actually is

An audit is a careful, structured check of a website against the WCAG standard from Module 8. You go through a site and find where real users hit barriers — the image with no description, the form field with no label, the button a keyboard can’t reach — then write up what’s wrong, which WCAG criterion it breaks, how serious it is, and how to fix it. Clients need this constantly, and it rewards careful, methodical work more than years of coding experience.

The credential: DHS Trusted Tester for Web

Your credential here is the Trusted Tester for Web certification from the U.S. Department of Homeland Security — and it’s free. It’s the federal gold standard for manual accessibility testing, the same process government agencies use.

Register at https://training.section508testing.net, then enroll in the Trusted Tester for Web track (currently version 5.1.3).

The honest picture: the training is self-paced and runs about 85 hours — the largest single block of work on this path. You learn the Section 508 standards, the testing tools, and the step-by-step process. The exam needs 85% to pass, and the certificate arrives within about 7–10 business days. Take the practice exams until you’re comfortably above 85% first.

The tool you’ll learn: ANDI

Trusted Tester teaches a free tool called ANDI, built by the U.S. Social Security Administration. It sits in your browser and lets you inspect a page the way assistive technology sees it — labels, headings, contrast, structure. None of it costs anything; the skill is knowing what to look for.

Optional, when you’re ready: CPACC

Once you’re working, you can add CPACC (Certified Professional in Accessibility Core Competencies) from the IAAP — a broader, knowledge-based certification, roughly $410 for members and $510 otherwise (check the IAAP site), with no experience requirement. A strong second credential — but Trusted Tester comes first, because it’s what gets you working.

Quick recap

An audit checks a website against WCAG and reports what blocks real users — your first paid service.

DHS Trusted Tester for Web is free, the federal gold standard, ~85 hours self-paced (85% to pass).

You’ll learn the free ANDI tool; CPACC is a strong optional second credential, later.
