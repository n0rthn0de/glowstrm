PART A · BUSINESS FORMATION · ~45 MINUTES

Setting Up Your Business

This is the module that takes you from “I have an idea” to “I have a registered business.” We’ll teach the landscape and the order of operations — and because the rules differ by state, you’ll do the specifics on your own state’s official portal. It’s more approachable than it sounds.

What an LLC is, and why it matters

LLC stands for Limited Liability Company. In plain terms, it puts a legal wall between your business and your personal life. If something ever went wrong with the business, that wall helps protect your personal savings, your car, your home. It also makes you look legitimate to clients and gives you a clean way to keep business money separate from personal money.

You can technically work as a “sole proprietor” with no formal setup — but then there’s no wall: you and the business are legally the same. Most people on this path start as a single-member LLC for exactly that liability protection. It’s a common, sensible starting point — but whether it’s right for you is a question for your own research and, ideally, a professional.

The general sequence

Here’s the order of operations almost every state follows. Learn it here, then do each step on your state’s portal:

Pick a business name and check it’s available in your state.

Choose a structure (most start as a single-member LLC).

File your formation paperwork with your state’s Secretary of State (filing fees commonly range from about $50 to $500).

Appoint a registered agent — who receives official mail for the business (you, or a paid service so your home address stays private).

Get a free EIN (your federal tax ID) from the IRS.

Open a separate business bank account.

Resources

  • SBA — Register your business (per-state lookup — makes the steps work anywhere). → https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/register-your-business
  • IRS — Apply for an EIN online (free; never pay a third party). → https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online

Quick recap

An LLC puts a legal wall between business and personal life — protection plus legitimacy.

The sequence: name → structure → file with the state → registered agent → free EIN → business bank account.

Rules differ by state; use your own Secretary of State’s portal, and get your EIN free from the IRS.
