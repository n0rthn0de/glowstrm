PART A · FINANCIAL LITERACY · ~40 MINUTES

Money, Taxes & Staying Legit

Money is the part new contractors most often get wrong — not because it’s hard, but because no one ever showed them the basics. This module shows you the categories and the habits. It does not set your numbers; a professional does that. The goal is to make you literate and a little less nervous.

Keep business and personal money separate

This is the first habit and the most important one. Run business income and expenses through a separate business bank account — not your personal checking. It makes taxes dramatically easier, strengthens the legal “wall” your LLC creates, and lets you actually see how the business is doing. Do it before your first invoice if you can.

Track what comes in and what goes out

Bookkeeping just means keeping a record of money in and money out — simple software or a careful spreadsheet to start. The point is that at tax time you’re not reconstructing a year from memory, and that legitimate business expenses (your Adobe Acrobat subscription, an exam fee, part of your internet bill) are written down, because they can reduce what you owe.

Understand self-employment tax — and set money aside

When you’re employed, your employer quietly pays half of your Social Security and Medicare taxes. When you work for yourself, you cover both halves — that’s “self-employment tax,” and it’s the surprise that catches new contractors off guard. Nothing is withheld from client payments, so you set money aside yourself, and you typically pay taxes during the year rather than all at once.

A common rule of thumb is to set aside 25–30% of your net income for taxes — but treat that as a placeholder, not a promise. Your CPA sets your real number, based on your income, your state, and your situation.
